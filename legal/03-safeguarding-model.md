# Safeguarding and service-boundary model

Status: draft operational model, 19 August 2026. It must be reviewed by a safeguarding professional and insurer, then rehearsed, before paid service begins. It does not turn Emotional Life Support into a crisis or clinical service.

## Scope

- Adults aged 18+ resident in England and Wales at launch.
- Non-clinical, person-led emotional support and bounded practical organisation.
- No emergency monitoring, diagnosis, therapy, domestic-abuse advocacy, mediation, regulated advice, case management, contact with a former partner, handling money/credentials, or communications in the client's name.
- Remote by default; in-person sessions only under the lone-working controls below.

## Entry and suitability

At the beginning of the free call:

1. confirm the person's name, age 18+, country and current location for the call;
2. explain scope, confidentiality limits, non-emergency status and data handling;
3. ask whether it is safe and private enough to talk, without displaying sensitive wording in reminders;
4. identify what support is being requested and whether a regulated/specialist professional is needed; and
5. do not take on a paid client until any identified safety boundary and referral plan is clear.

Avoid blanket exclusion based on a diagnosis or disability. Assess whether this particular service can be delivered safely, consider reasonable adjustments, and record objective reasons for a decision.

## Triage model

### Green — within scope

Distress associated with transition; no indication of immediate serious harm; client can engage, make their own decisions and use ordinary support routes. Continue within the agreed offer and review suitability regularly.

### Amber — support only with a plan

Examples include worsening mental health, thoughts of self-harm without apparent immediate intent, current coercive control or stalking, substance misuse affecting participation, acute housing/financial instability, repeated boundary pressure, or a need beyond competence. Pause ordinary content, check immediate safety, encourage/agree contact with an appropriate regulated or specialist service, consult the safeguarding lead/supervisor, and decide whether ELS can continue alongside that help. Record facts and decisions.

### Red — ordinary support stops

Examples include credible immediate danger, stated plan/means/intent to cause serious harm, an unfolding medical emergency, serious concern about a child or adult at risk, or violence/stalking requiring emergency intervention. Stay calm, explain the limit of confidentiality where possible, obtain/confirm location, contact 999 or the appropriate authority when necessary, share only what is needed, do not physically intervene, and record the timeline and rationale. Conduct an incident review before resuming or closing.

This model is not a clinical risk-scoring tool. Training must cover how to ask simple direct safety questions, avoid overpromising secrecy, and hand over to qualified services.

## Confidentiality decision test

Before disclosure, where time permits, record:

1. What specific harm or legal duty is involved?
2. Is the risk credible, serious and sufficiently imminent, or is a safeguarding/legal duty engaged?
3. Can the client make the contact themselves or consent to a supported referral?
4. What is the minimum information and minimum recipient needed?
5. Is it safe and lawful to tell the client before sharing?
6. What was shared, with whom, when and why?

Routine supervision should use anonymised information wherever possible. Confidentiality does not create legal professional privilege.

## Domestic-abuse boundary

- Do not advise on a personalised safety plan unless trained and insured to do so; refer to a specialist service.
- Ask whether digital communications and voicemail are safe. Use neutral appointment wording.
- Never contact, warn, investigate or mediate with the alleged perpetrator.
- Do not keep secret copies of evidence for the client or advise how to gather evidence.
- Avoid joint sessions where abuse/coercion is alleged.
- Use GOV.UK specialist routes and 999 for immediate danger.

## Children and adults at risk

ELS is adult-only, but clients may disclose information about children or adults at risk. Obtain jurisdiction-specific safeguarding advice, identify the designated safeguarding lead and deputy, keep local authority contact routes, document the concern in the person's words, avoid investigative questioning, and make a referral where the legal/policy threshold is met.

## Boundaries and dependency controls

- One written offer with the monthly price, agreed scope and clear availability boundaries.
- Rolling monthly renewal with 30 days' written notice to end. Review suitability regularly.
- Administrative response hours stated in writing; never describe messaging as crisis support.
- No personal social-media contact, gifts of significant value, loans, sexual/romantic relationship, shared accommodation or dual business relationship.
- No “unlimited access”, “always available” or exclusivity claims.
- Maintain an up-to-date referral directory and closing plan.
- Record and discuss repeated boundary crossings in supervision.

## Lone working and in-person sessions

- Use a professional, accessible venue; do not start with client homes or isolated settings.
- Keep a calendar, check-in/check-out contact and agreed overdue escalation.
- Complete a dynamic safety check; leave if conduct, intoxication or location creates risk.
- Do not transport clients in a personal vehicle at launch.
- Keep emergency phone access and never disclose a home address unnecessarily.

## Governance required before paid launch

- named safeguarding lead and deputy/cover arrangement;
- enhanced safeguarding, suicide-awareness and domestic-abuse awareness training appropriate to a non-clinical role;
- at least monthly professional supervision, plus ad hoc access after incidents;
- professional indemnity, public liability, cyber/data and, if relevant, employers' liability insurance explicitly covering the described service;
- incident, complaint, referral, lone-working, whistleblowing and data-breach procedures;
- professional review of Article 9/10 information handling; and
- quarterly review during the pilot, including near misses and boundary pressure.
