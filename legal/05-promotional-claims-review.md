# Promotional claims review

Status: site and campaign review, 19 August 2026. Re-review every landing page, ad, social post, email and testimonial before publication.

## Overall conclusion

Position the service as non-clinical human support based on lived and listening experience. Avoid treatment, recovery, safety, confidentiality and outcome guarantees. Keep evidence for every objective biographical, price and credential claim.

## Site claims reviewed

| Claim/theme | Decision | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| “Emotional support through the end of an unhealthy relationship” | Keep | Describes service context, not diagnosis. Do not label a named partner abusive or disordered. |
| “The care and honesty of a good friend” | Keep with scope nearby | It is an analogy; avoid implying friendship, unlimited access or a personal relationship outside contract. |
| “Understands difficult divorce first-hand” | Keep if accurate | Evidence is Chris's own experience. Make clear that another person's experience may differ. |
| “Private and confidential” | Amend | Use “private conversation” and disclose serious-safety, safeguarding and legal limits. Never promise absolute confidentiality. |
| “Secure booking page” | Remove unless verified | Use “HubSpot booking page”. HTTPS alone is not enough to make a broad security claim. |
| “Find peace… confidence… hope” | Keep only as aspirations | Add that these are aims, results vary, and no outcome is guaranteed. Avoid “will”, “transform”, “heal”, “recover” or success percentages without robust evidence. |
| “Make clearer decisions / avoid cost and stress” | Use as personal belief only in Chris's story | Do not claim the paid service saves legal fees, improves settlements or prevents stress without suitable evidence. |
| ANDYSMANCLUB role | Keep only while current and evidenced | Preserve role confirmation and check the organisation's brand/name rules. Add that it does not provide or endorse ELS. |
| Samaritans training/role | Keep only in exact current tense | Verify status before every campaign. Do not use logo or imply Samaritans accreditation/endorsement. |
| “Five two-hour sessions each week” | Keep for Enhanced only | State the agreed monthly cadence. Do not turn it into an “always available” claim. |
| £1,200 / £3,000 / £10,000 per month | Keep with qualifications | State that prices include VAT, the agreement rolls monthly and 30 days' written notice is needed to end it. |
| “On-demand support” or “priority response” | Keep with scope nearby | State that the service is not emergency support and that availability is agreed as part of the monthly arrangement. |
| “No pressure” | Keep only if operationally true | The free call must not use high-pressure urgency, fear or a same-call discount. |
| “No preparation” | Keep | Booking form should not request a detailed story. Give privacy/scope notice at the start. |

## Prohibited or high-risk campaign themes

- Do not target ads using inferred mental health, domestic-abuse, divorce-case or other sensitive categories.
- Do not say “therapy without the waiting list”, “treat anxiety/depression/trauma”, “suicide prevention”, “safe from abuse”, “expert”, “accredited”, “qualified counsellor/coach”, or “proven” unless the facts and evidence change and professional review approves the claim.
- Do not manufacture scarcity. Genuine capacity limits can be stated factually and kept current.
- Do not imply affiliation with the NHS, Samaritans, ANDYSMANCLUB, courts, solicitors or domestic-abuse services.
- Do not use a client's story, screenshot, message or outcome as a testimonial without specific, optional and informed permission. An incentive must be disclosed, and exceptional outcomes must not be presented as typical.
- Do not use “before/after” emotional-health transformations or guarantee reconciliation, settlement, recovery, peace, confidence or reduced legal fees.
- Do not omit VAT, the period, material limitations, cancellation conditions or unavoidable travel charges from price-led ads.

## Safer campaign language

Use: “A private, non-clinical place to talk and think through what comes next”; “support informed by lived experience”; “your decisions remain yours”; “rolling monthly support”; “prices include VAT”; and “not an emergency or regulated advice service.”

## Approval checklist for each promotion

1. Identify every factual, comparative, price, credential and outcome claim.
2. File dated evidence before publication.
3. Put qualifications where the claim appears, not behind a distant link.
4. Check the audience and targeting do not infer sensitive data.
5. Check total VAT-inclusive price and material limits are prominent.
6. Check images/testimonials have permission and are not misleading.
7. Confirm current capacity, role titles, availability and offer version.
8. Archive the final promotion, evidence and approval date.

Reference points: [CAP Code section 3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), [ASA mental-health treatment claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/mental-health-treatment-claims.html), and [ICO direct-marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/).
