The relationship may be over.

Your mind is still trying to make sense of it.

You may replay conversations, search for the moment things changed, or wonder whether it was really as bad as it felt. Confusion does not mean you made the wrong decision.

You’re grieving more than the relationship.

There is the person, but also the future you kept trying to make real, the routines you shared, the version of them you hoped would return—and the version of you who kept believing it could work.

You keep replaying it

You revisit arguments, apologies and good moments, trying to decide which version was real.

Relief and grief arrive together

You may breathe more easily one moment, then ache for them—or for what you wanted it to be—the next.

You don’t trust yourself

After having your feelings questioned or your boundaries moved, even small choices can feel unsafe.

Other people don’t quite understand

They ask why you stayed, why you miss them, or why you aren’t happier now. You are tired of defending a reality you are still trying to understand.

The ending keeps interrupting your life

Messages, money, housing, belongings, children or mutual friends can keep pulling you back into it.

The future feels blank

You wanted freedom from what was hurting. You may not yet know what you want freedom for.

The relationship can end and still reach into every corner of your life.

What happened does not stay neatly in the past just because the relationship has finished.

At work

You read the same sentence three times. You worry people can see you slipping. Small requests feel enormous because so much of your attention is still elsewhere.

At home

Silence can feel peaceful and unbearable. Ordinary tasks keep coming while the home itself holds memories, absences and unfinished decisions.

With other people

You cancel plans, over-explain, go quiet—or say you’re fine because the whole truth feels too complicated and you no longer know who will believe you.

In your body

Sleep is lighter. Your shoulders stay tense. Your body can remain on alert even when the room is quiet.

In your decisions

You want certainty before you move, but the relationship taught you to second-guess what you know.

In the way you see yourself

You wonder how you became smaller, quieter or less certain—and whether the person you were before is still there.

The hardest part may be that everyone expects you to move on while part of you is still working out what was real.

Emotional Life Support is one-to-one, non-clinical support after the end of an unhealthy relationship. We make room for the contradiction and confusion, then gently turn the tangle into choices, connection and manageable next steps.

  1. 01

    Feel heard

    Tell the whole, complicated truth without being judged, rushed or told what you should have done.

  2. 02

    Find clarity

    Separate what happened, what you were told and what you know until your own perspective is easier to trust.

  3. 03

    Rebuild at your pace

    Choose the next step that is genuinely yours, reconnect with life beyond the relationship, and follow through.

Peer-to-peer, not top-down

Because being understood can be the beginning of movement.

You may be wondering

Do I need to know exactly what I want help with?

No. Not being able to explain the whole problem is often part of the problem. We can begin with what feels hardest today.

Is this therapy?

No. This is peer-to-peer, non-clinical support. It does not diagnose or treat mental health conditions and is not a substitute for therapy, domestic-abuse advocacy or crisis care.

What do you mean by social prescribing?

It means looking beyond conversation alone and helping you connect with non-medical community support—such as a relevant group, activity, practical service or source of belonging. Emotional Life Support is not an NHS social-prescribing service and does not make clinical referrals.

What happens in the free session?

We slow things down, talk about what is happening, and see whether this kind of support feels useful. You do not need to prepare, and there is no obligation to continue.

What if I need specialist help?

I will be honest about the limits of my role and help you find an appropriate therapist, solicitor, financial adviser, medical professional or specialist service where needed.

What happens to the information I share?

What you share is treated with care and used only to understand your enquiry and provide the support you request. Before publishing this page, a full privacy notice and secure booking system will be connected here.
